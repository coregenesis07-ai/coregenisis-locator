2026 Sentencing Guidelines are now effective

Effective November 1, 2026: The U.S. Sentencing Commission's 2026 Guidelines Manual incorporates amendments 837 through 843. The Commission now identifies the 2026 amendments as effective, so older wording that they were merely “scheduled” for November 1 is outdated.

Amendment 841 reorganizes Chapter Five's presentation of sentencing options and adds §5A1.1, which explains the Sentencing Table and identifies sentencing options associated with Zones A, B, C, and D. The Commission emphasizes that sentencing courts must consider available sentencing options, subject to statutory limitations and the facts of the individual case.

Important: An expanded or clarified guideline sentencing option does not guarantee probation, home detention, a split sentence, or any particular outcome. Statutory restrictions, guideline calculations, criminal history, mandatory minimums, 18 U.S.C. § 3553(a), and the sentencing judge's determination still matter.

Las Guías de Sentencia de 2026 ya están vigentes

Desde el 1 de noviembre de 2026, el Manual de Guías de 2026 incorpora las enmiendas 837 a 843. La Enmienda 841 reorganiza la presentación de las opciones de sentencia en el Capítulo Cinco y añade §5A1.1 sobre la Tabla de Sentencias y las Zonas A, B, C y D.

Esto no garantiza libertad condicional, detención domiciliaria, una sentencia dividida ni ningún resultado específico. Siguen aplicando las restricciones legales, el cálculo de las guías, antecedentes penales, mínimos obligatorios, 18 U.S.C. § 3553(a) y la decisión del juez.

2026 First Step Act Time Credits rule

BOP's 2026 interim final rule revises 28 CFR 523.42(a) so the regulatory text states that an eligible inmate begins earning FSA Time Credits after the inmate's term of imprisonment commences. The prior parenthetical tying commencement to arrival or voluntary surrender at the designated BOP facility was removed.

The change does not make FTC automatic at sentencing. Statutory eligibility and successful participation in qualifying assigned programming or productive activities remain important, and the rules for earning credits are distinct from the requirements for applying earned credits.

Source discrepancy to know: BOP's public FAQ currently still displays older commencement wording. When the commencement date is material, consult the newer Federal Register rule and qualified counsel rather than relying on the older FAQ sentence alone.

Regla 2026 sobre Créditos de Tiempo FSA

La regla final interina de 2026 revisa 28 CFR 523.42(a) para indicar que una persona elegible comienza a ganar créditos después de que comience su término de encarcelamiento. Se eliminó el texto que vinculaba expresamente ese comienzo a la llegada o entrega voluntaria en la institución designada.

Los créditos no se vuelven automáticos por ese cambio: la elegibilidad, la participación exitosa y las reglas separadas para aplicar los créditos siguen siendo importantes.

First Step Act aggregation case: Supreme Court review denied

Current status: In Bonnie v. Dunbar, No. 24-6665, the Fourth Circuit affirmed the denial of habeas relief on November 5, 2025. The Supreme Court docket for No. 25-1363 shows that the petition for a writ of certiorari was denied October 5, 2026.

The Fourth Circuit majority treated consecutive sentences as a single aggregate term for the disputed FSA Time Credit eligibility question; Judge Wynn dissented. The Supreme Court's denial of certiorari does not itself create a new merits ruling or endorse every part of the lower court's reasoning, but it means the Court did not take this case for review.

For families: People serving consecutive federal sentences where one conviction is FSA-ineligible should not rely on older reports saying the Supreme Court petition is still pending. Individual eligibility remains fact-specific; preserve sentencing records, BOP calculations, and administrative-remedy records and seek qualified legal advice when needed.

Current BOP FSA guidance: earning vs. applying credits

BOP guidance distinguishes between earning Federal Time Credits through qualifying assigned programming and applying earned credits toward prerelease custody or supervised release. Eligibility to earn, the amount earned, and whether credits can be applied are separate questions.

Ganar vs. aplicar créditos

Ganar Créditos Federales de Tiempo y poder aplicarlos a custodia previa a la liberación o libertad supervisada son cuestiones distintas. La situación individual y los requisitos legales importan.

28 U.S.C. § 2255: challenging a federal conviction or sentence

Section 2255 generally permits a person in federal custody to ask the sentencing court to vacate, set aside, or correct a sentence on specified grounds. It generally contains a one-year limitations period, but the statute provides four possible starting points. The correct deadline depends on the facts of the individual case, and second or successive motions face additional restrictions.

La sección 2255 permite, en determinadas circunstancias, solicitar al tribunal que impuso la sentencia que la anule, deje sin efecto o corrija. Los plazos y requisitos dependen de los hechos del caso.

Facility status and closure claims: verify the live BOP directory

Current check — November 2, 2026: BOP's live location directory continues to list Morgantown FPC, Duluth FPC, Beaumont, Big Spring, La Tuna, Lexington, and Petersburg among current BOP locations. Families should not treat an older closure or realignment notice as proof that a facility is currently closed.

An older January 2025 RDAP locations document labeled some programs or institutions as “closing” or “suspended,” but BOP later announced that the prior directive to deactivate FPC Duluth was rescinded. Program status and institution status are also not the same thing. Before travel, mail, or transfer planning, verify the institution's current BOP page and location directory.
