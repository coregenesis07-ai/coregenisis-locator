Good Conduct Time vs. FSA Time Credits

A family-friendly guide to two different federal credit systems. Official sources reviewed October 21, 2026.

Important: This is an independent educational resource, not BOP or DOJ. It is not legal advice and does not calculate an official release date. BOP's official sentence computation, eligibility decisions, risk assessments, credit calculations, and placement decisions control.

The key difference

Good Conduct Time (GCT)

Under 18 U.S.C. § 3624(b), a qualifying federal prisoner serving more than one year (other than life) may receive up to 54 days for each year of the sentence imposed, subject to the statute and BOP determination.

First Step Act Time Credits (FTC)

Under 18 U.S.C. § 3632(d)(4), an eligible prisoner earns FTC for successful participation in evidence-based recidivism reduction programming or productive activities. Earned credits are applied toward prerelease custody or supervised release subject to the separate requirements of 18 U.S.C. § 3624(g).

FTC: earning is not the same as application

There are two different questions: Is the person eligible to earn FTC? and Can earned FTC be applied toward prerelease custody or supervised release? The statute contains offense-based earning exclusions, while 18 U.S.C. § 3624(g) sets additional requirements for application. Individual circumstances can also affect application, so an earned-credit balance alone does not establish a release or placement date.

Successful participation matters

The statute awards FTC for successful participation in qualifying evidence-based recidivism reduction programming or productive activities. BOP policy and guidance govern how participation is recorded and administered.

PATTERN / risk findings matter when credits are applied

For prerelease custody, § 3624(g) generally requires minimum/low risk on the last two reassessments, unless the statutory Warden-approval pathway is satisfied. The statute provides a related risk requirement for early transfer to supervised release. These are application rules, not a promise of a particular placement.

The 12-month limit needs careful wording

The 12-month statutory limit applies to an FTC-based early transfer to begin supervised release under § 3624(g)(3). It should not be described as a blanket 365-day cap on all FTC use, because FTC can also be applied toward prerelease custody when the governing requirements are met.

Resumen en español

GCT y los créditos de tiempo de la FSA no son lo mismo. Bajo 18 U.S.C. § 3624(b), una persona federal que califica y cumple una sentencia de más de un año (que no sea cadena perpetua) puede recibir hasta 54 días por cada año de la sentencia impuesta, sujeto a la ley y a la determinación del BOP.

Bajo 18 U.S.C. § 3632(d)(4), una persona elegible gana 10 días de FTC por cada 30 días de participación exitosa en programas o actividades que califican. Una persona que BOP determine de riesgo mínimo o bajo y que no haya aumentado su riesgo durante dos evaluaciones consecutivas gana 5 días adicionales por cada 30 días.

Ganar créditos y aplicarlos son cuestiones distintas. La aplicación hacia custodia previa a la liberación o libertad supervisada está sujeta a requisitos adicionales de 18 U.S.C. § 3624(g). El límite de 12 meses se refiere a una transferencia anticipada para comenzar libertad supervisada basada en FTC; no debe describirse como un límite general para todo uso de FTC.
