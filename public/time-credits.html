Good Conduct Time vs. FSA Time Credits

A family-friendly guide to two different federal credit systems. Updated for current BOP guidance reviewed October 21, 2026.

Important: This is an independent educational resource, not BOP or DOJ. It is not legal advice and does not calculate an official release date. BOP's official sentence computation, eligibility decisions, risk assessments, credit calculations, and placement decisions control.

The key difference

Good Conduct Time (GCT)

For qualifying federal inmates, GCT can be earned at up to 54 days for each year of the sentence imposed, subject to the governing law and BOP calculation.

First Step Act Time Credits (FTC)

Eligible inmates can earn FTC through successful participation in assigned evidence-based recidivism reduction programs and productive activities. FTC may be applied toward prerelease custody or, when requirements are met, earlier transfer to supervised release.

FTC: earning is not the same as application

A person may need to answer two separate questions: Can I earn FTC? and Can my earned FTC be applied now? BOP's current FAQ describes separate eligibility and risk-related requirements for application. A final order of removal can also affect application even where credits may be earned.

Successful participation matters

BOP describes successful participation as attending required sessions (with excused absences handled under program rules), completing assigned activities, and following program requirements. Certain statuses or declining recommended programming can affect earning.

Credits can be lost and may be restored

BOP states earned FTC can be lost for misconduct or program/activity rule violations and describes a case-by-case restoration process after qualifying clear conduct.

PATTERN / risk level matters

BOP's FAQ states minimum/low risk findings are important to ordinary FTC application. Medium/high risk individuals may have a separate pathway involving a Warden determination under the applicable requirements.

Resumen en español

GCT y los créditos de tiempo de la FSA no son lo mismo. Para personas federales que califican, el GCT puede ser de hasta 54 días por cada año de la sentencia impuesta. Los créditos FTC de la FSA dependen de elegibilidad y participación exitosa en programas o actividades asignadas que califican.

En general, una persona elegible puede ganar 10 días de FTC por cada 30 días de participación exitosa. Bajo las condiciones legales aplicables de riesgo mínimo/bajo, pueden corresponder 5 días adicionales. Ganar créditos no significa automáticamente que todos puedan aplicarse inmediatamente.

La aplicación puede ser hacia custodia previa a la liberación y, cuando se cumplen los requisitos, hacia una transferencia anticipada a libertad supervisada. BOP indica que la transferencia anticipada a libertad supervisada basada en FTC no puede exceder 12 meses.
